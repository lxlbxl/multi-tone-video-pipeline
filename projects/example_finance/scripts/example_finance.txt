One surprise car repair is the reason most people's investing plan falls apart. The pain isn't the eight hundred dollars. It's that the money comes out of an investment account, often at a bad time, and sometimes with a tax bill on top. An emergency fund fixes this by sitting between your life and your long-term money. Three to six months of essential expenses, in a separate high-yield savings account, untouched. When the car breaks, you pay from the buffer, and your invested money keeps compounding, undisturbed. Historically, staying invested through a downturn has mattered far more to long-run returns than a few extra contributions. So the sequence is simple. Build the buffer first. Then invest, and leave it alone. Automate one transfer to a separate savings account this week, and let it grow before you do anything else.
